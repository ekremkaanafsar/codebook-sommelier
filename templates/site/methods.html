Methods

How the {{ n_rows }} entries were found, checked and scored, and what the scores can and cannot tell you.

Discovery

Candidates were gathered in five tiers, one subfield at a time. Tier A: research guides written by university librarians in political science, statistics, public opinion, elections, international relations and area studies. Tier B: curated meta-lists and data hubs, and R and Python data packages. Tier C: data repositories such as Harvard Dataverse, ICPSR, GESIS, the UK Data Service and Zenodo. Tier D: replication archives and data articles in the discipline's main journals. Tier E: official producers, including the United Nations system, the World Bank, the IMF, the OECD and electoral bodies. Every guide that lists a dataset is recorded, and the number of distinct university guides is shown on each dataset page as a rough measure of how widely it is recommended.

Inclusion criteria

An entry must be systematically collected and documented, with a codebook or methods paper, and be used or usable in peer-reviewed political science research. Open, registration-only, restricted and commercial data are all included, with the access level stated. One-off replication files with no general reuse value and undocumented scrapes are left out. Discontinued datasets stay listed with that status, and a successor is named where there is one. Data portals and licensed products are included as their own resource types; library databases and archives of primary documents are kept apart in the annex tables: literature databases and primary-source archives.

Verification rules

  • Each address, DOI, version and coverage figure was taken from an official page opened while compiling the entry, never from memory.
  • A value that could not be confirmed on such a page is marked as not confirmed, with a note saying why.
  • Where the landing page and the codebook disagree, both values are reported.
  • The dataset is kept distinct from its reference article, and the version most cited in the literature is flagged when it is not the latest.
  • Methodological critiques are recorded only when they can be attributed to a published source.
  • Descriptions are written in the compiler's own words, not copied from the producers' sites.

Link checking

Every landing, download and codebook address is requested with an ordinary browser identification, following redirects, with a 20-second timeout and one retry. The result is shown on the dataset page next to the landing address: working, redirects, blocked by the site, or broken. A refusal such as 401, 403 or 429 is recorded as "blocked by site", not as broken, because many producers turn away automated requests; those links need a manual check. Dataset files themselves are never downloaded.

Limits of the scores

  • The scores are noisy. They were assigned in batches, and batch effects are likely: similar evidence may have been scored a little differently in different batches.
  • Validation by human reviewers is pending. Until it is done the scores are provisional and should be read as a first screen, not a verdict.
  • The overall score is the unweighted mean of the eight dimensions, shown for convenience. It is not a ranking, and the site never sorts datasets by it.
